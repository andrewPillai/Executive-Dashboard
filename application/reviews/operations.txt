OPERATIONS DEPARTMENT HEAD — SYNTHETIC END-USER REVIEW
Review date: 2026-10-05
Basis: source inspection of frontend/src/App.tsx, BusinessDetails.tsx, backend/depth.py and main.py, plus read-only queries of data/northstar.sqlite and dashboard.json. No shared browser interaction. These are role hypotheses, not validated Avantor requirements or interviews.

VERDICT
Useful for explaining a warehouse exception and following a proposed response. Not yet sufficient to run the daily warehouse management meeting: the default working queue cannot tell me which facility must do what today, by when, or whether it can deliver.

WHAT IS RELEVANT
- Operations metrics deliberately exclude direct fulfillment from stock backlog. On-hand inventory value and zero-stock positions have honest definitions.
- Product detail connects inventory at named facilities to customer orders. STOCK-003 is a credible imbalance story: local zero stock and 500 units elsewhere.
- The case ties Operations, Planning & Sourcing, Customer Service and Finance together; customer/product/manufacturer links support investigation.
- Explicit human approval and the distinction between assignment and resolution are appropriate. The demo does not pretend to execute SAP movements.

TOP FIVE GAPS, IN PRIORITY ORDER
1. P1 — No actionable daily facility queue.
Evidence: depth.py operations queue selects all STOCK lines with unshipped quantity and sorts the latest order date first, limited to 10. BusinessDetails Queue shows order/customer/product/fulfillment/value/scenario, but not warehouse, open units, requested date, committed date or days overdue. App.tsx Orders omits these columns too. Latest order is not necessarily most urgent.
Need: facility and due/overdue filters; outstanding units; promised/requested dates; overdue age; explicit priority logic such as overdue first, then due today. Keep monetary exposure as secondary context.
Acceptance: a manager selects Houston, sees STOCK-003 line 299995 with 90 units outstanding and requested date 2026-09-25, and can inspect why it outranks future-due work as of 2026-10-01.

2. P1 — Queue drill-through loses its operating scope.
Evidence: DepartmentWorkbench's Open order explorer link is always /orders. Orders supports customer/product/manufacturer scope, but no warehouse, fulfillment-mode or backlog predicate. Ten selected warehouse backlog records therefore lead into every order line, including direct shipments.
Need: preserve STOCK plus unshipped filters when opening the complete queue; carry site/date filters in the URL and show scope/count clearly. Direct shipments can be a separately labeled coordination view.
Acceptance: workbench and full explorer show the same backlog total and predicate; pagination never introduces delivered or DIRECT lines into the stock backlog.

3. P1 — Available stock and transfer feasibility are not established.
Evidence: product inventory displays on_hand_qty only; inventory_balances contains opening and on-hand quantities but no allocation, reservations or transfer commitments. No transit time, shipment cutoffs or transfer cost are modeled. STOCK-003 recommendation correctly says check feasibility, but 500 on hand cannot be treated as 500 available.
Need: show unallocated/available units, source commitments, proposed transfer units and earliest delivery date; seed these explicitly, or mark feasibility unknown and require verification. Never automatically promise a transfer from on-hand alone.
Acceptance: Houston's 90-unit shortfall links to Miami's 500 on hand, with a clear availability/ETA check before approving a transfer proposal.

4. P2 — No facility performance or capacity view.
Evidence: Operations inherits the shared invoiced-sales trend and overall delivery OTIF. Network page lists facility names/countries only. No site-level backlog age, warehouse OTIF, pick/pack throughput, labor/dock capacity or carrier cutoff exists in the inspected model.
Need: start with derivable warehouse-specific backlog/late shipment trends; add explicitly synthetic capacity facts only when modeled. Separate DIRECT delivery performance from warehouse accountability. Do not infer warehouse performance from company-wide OTIF.
Acceptance: compare Houston and Miami on overdue stock units and warehouse delivery performance; identify capacity as modeled data or an explicit unknown.

5. P2 — Assignment cannot yet become coordinated execution.
Evidence: main.py draft has a single accountable department and three generic text steps. UI accepts department and due date; workflow ends at Assigned. There are no individual task owners, transfer proposal details, dependencies, progress updates or resolution evidence.
Need: an editable action plan with warehouse transfer check, Planning availability confirmation and Customer Service revised commitment; each has owner/due/status/dependency. Preserve human approval and do not call assignment issue resolution.
Acceptance: STOCK-003 can be assigned with source/destination/product/quantity references and required checks; the issue stays open until transfer/delivery evidence is recorded.
